TL;DR

FICO Feature Management Aggregation replaces complex coding scripts with a guided UI for banking and risk teams. It simplifies configuring real-time data features and advanced time windows without needing data engineers. This enables non-technical users to deploy critical fraud-detection formulas in seconds instead of weeks.

As Lead Product Designer, I designed the end-to-end UI for Aggregation, turning complex data requirements into an intuitive, scalable framework.

overview

Feature Management is a centralized data platform that allows banks to instantly track customer behavior and easily create data formulas to predict risk, stop fraud, and improve customer service in real time.

For the aggregation capability specifically, the UI replaces complex coding scripts with a simple form. By separating data setup from mathematical logic, the UI guides analysts step-by-step through configuring advanced time windows in a clear, linear flow.

For example, tracking a specific event over a rolling 30-second window.

my contribution

I partnered closely with the Feature Management Product Manager, the Front-End Architect, and the engineering team to design the ecosystem's first core capability: Aggregation.

impact

Speed

Eliminated weeks of cross-team coordination, enabling users to start and set up their own schemas in seconds.

Design Scalability

Empowered non-technical and technical users with the ability to configure complex data structures instantly, without needing a solution consultant.

Engineering Efficiency

Product teams can embed and drop the component directly into their unique host environments with zero adjustments required.

the backstory

problem

Fraud & risk teams depended on data engineers to build features that extract real-time activity data needed to spot fraud. This technical process would take days or weeks, making critical data hard to access and slowing down response times.

process
Early Wireframes & Requirements

For this initiative, the collaboration structure was unique. The PM was more hands-on, providing initial conceptual sketches of their vision for the feature management workflow. I took these functional requirements and translated them into an intuitive, scalable user experience.

I started off by asking the foundational questions:

  • Why are we building this?
  • Who are our users? Why do they need this?
  • What workflows are they trying to accomplish?

I based the initial layout iteration on a design template I created for the Business Terms Capability (BTC) project. This phase was highly experimental, as the product team was still deciding on the core navigation structure — specifically whether to use a hybrid top-and-side navigation or a sidebar-only model.
