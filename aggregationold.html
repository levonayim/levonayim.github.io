TL;DR

Fraud & risk teams depended on data engineers to build features that extract real-time activity data needed to spot fraud. This technical process would take days or weeks, making critical data hard to access and slowing down response times. I led and designed the end-to-end experience for FICO's Feature Management platform, encompassing its core data transformation (aggregations, expressions), state management (stored values, KPIs), and operational serving (decision stores, hotlists).

context

Feature Management is a centralized data platform that allows banks to instantly track customer behaviour and easily create data formulas to predict risk, stop fraud, and improve customer service in real time.

For the aggregation capability specifically, the UI replaces complex coding scripts with a simple form. By separating data setup from mathematical logic, the UI guides analysts step-by-step through configuring advanced time windows (e.g., tracking a specific event over a rolling 30-second window) in a clear, linear flow.

process

I partnered closely with the Feature Management (FM) Product Manager, the Front-End Architect, and the engineering team to design the ecosystem's first core capability: Aggregation.

I started off by asking the foundational questions:

  • Why are we building this?
  • Who are our users? Why do they need this?
  • What workflows are they trying to accomplish?

For this initiative, the collaboration structure was unique. The PM was more hands-on, providing initial conceptual sketches of their vision for the feature management workflow. I took these functional requirements and translated them into an intuitive, scalable user experience.

I based the initial layout iteration on a design template I created for the Business Terms Capability (BTC) project. This phase was highly experimental, as the product team was still deciding on the core navigation structure — specifically whether to use a hybrid top-and-side navigation or a sidebar-only model.
