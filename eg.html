overview

The Experience Gateway serves as the central entry point for every user archetype on the FICO Platform's. Its primary objective is to onboard new users smoothly while simplifying daily tasks for returning power users. However, given its high traffic and broad scope, the gateway risks overwhelming users if role-specific value is not prioritized.

To solve this, the gateway must first address Platform Administrators, who are the prerequisite for all downstream users. Without seamless admin provisioning and schema migration, no other user archetypes can access or configure their workspaces.

my contribution

Following a one-week platform discovery workshop in Los Angeles, project momentum stalled as teams decentralized. To drive progress, I:

the backstory

problem

High-traffic homepages often overwhelm users with role-irrelevant data, making a clear entry point essential. Due to initial release constraints, the team prioritized manual admin setup to establish core access control rather than building automated sales pipeline ingestion.

process
Phase 1 — Discovery Workshop Synthesis

This initiative began with an intensive workshop in Los Angeles as our VP of Architecture brought together our internal team and a partner design agency to dissect the friction points of FICO's legacy Decision Management Platform (DMP) and the kickoff to the new FICO Platform.

  • Focus & Priority — Defined the foundational Day 0 and Day 1 onboarding user journey.
  • Stakeholders — Partnered with Solutions Architects and leadership to define and understand our primary target archetypes and their needs.
  • Task — Map the primary archetype's user journey, list key Jobs-to-be-Done, and design the experience to ensure immediate product value on day one.

During the workshop, the VP of Architecture established our primary target audience for the initial release would be the Platform Administrators on both the FICO and client side, which then became the main focus for the designs.

Admins were the foundation of the ecosystem; without them successfully migrating and importing data, other user archetypes couldn't use the platform at all.

Phase 2 — Post-Workshop Synthesis

Following the Los Angeles workshop, the team temporarily dispersed back to their day-to-day projects.

Recognizing the tight timeline and high stakes of the initiative, I took the initiative to map out an initial end-to-end flow. I knew that having a tangible baseline, even an early draft, would serve as a conversation starter to reignite our momentum.

Using low-fidelity sketches, I brought a concrete visual starting point to our Product Manager to reignite the product definition phase.

Together, we translated these early flows into a structured list of core user journeys, categorizing features from the very beginning of the FICO Admin user journey, to the Client's Admin's user journey, to each archetype's landing page and what they would see. We prioritized immediate product needs versus future enhancements.

Phase 3 — Research & Validation through FICO World 2025

While I didn't attend the conference in person, I led the UX preparation alongside the Product Manager and Front-End Architect to determine which screens would drive the most impact on stage.

Optimizing for high-value feedback and tight presentation windows, we decided to narrow our showcase to the landing pages of three distinct archetypes instead of the whole user flow of the onboarding journey. As the landing pages are the areas with the most diverse information and would be the most revisited pages based on each archetype, we wanted to make sure we were able to capture deep, actionable insights on the core platform that benefits the experience from each landing page.

Feedback from FICO World 2025

Service Creator

  • 90% of participants found the page visually overwhelming and mentally demanding
  • Unclear about the purpose or value of certain elements
  • Users expect more personalized and role-relevant views

Business Analyst

  • Most participants identified KPls and performance metrics as the most essential component of the page, highlighting the importance of tracking business outcomes
  • Users were most interested in metrics that track performance over time
  • Strong demand for self-service insights and flexible dashboards

Solution Creator

  • Most participants (47%) identified Assets as the most essential component of the page, followed by Business Goals & KPIs and Relationship Map
  • Wanted improvements like more dynamic data and zooming capability
  • 27% of participants found the page clear, with 3 explicitly saying "No" confusion
Things to consider

Lack of Role Attribution

  • Responses were not tagged by user role, so it's unclear which insights came from which types of users (e.g., Ops vs. Product Owners)

No User Context or Environment Info

  • We don't know the scale, complexity, or tech stack of the environments these users operate in, which can affect their needs.

Focus on Feature Requests

  • Scope of the research was limited to product research rather than user research - next gen features
