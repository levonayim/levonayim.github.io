TL;DR

FICO is undergoing a massive strategic transformation, evolving from a legacy financial data analytics tool into an adaptable, multi-sector B2B Platform Builder, also known as the new FICO Platform. However, internal product teams were still thinking in legacy silos, creating a critical gap between this complex new architecture and the teams' collective understanding.

As a self-initiated initiative, I conducted technical stakeholder research and designed the platform's first visual end-to-end blueprint and mental model framework.

overview

When teams were starting to transition to the new FICO Platform's, everyone was heads-down on their own assigned capability. There was no shared visual documentation of how the platform was structured, or how individual capabilities were connected within the larger system. Furthermore, the underlying architectural diagrams and engineering terminology were so deeply technical that the platform was incredibly difficult to grasp or visualize.

In the midst of a crunch deadline, no one had the time or space to stop and ask the foundational question:

Does everyone actually understand how this entire platform ecosystem fit together?

Teams were building in parallel isolation without a map.

I identified this gap and took ownership of solving it by conducting stakeholder research, synthesizing platform-level knowledge, and producing the first visual documentation of the platform's end-to-end flow.

my contribution

As the sole driver of this initiative, I took ownership of defining the scope, interviewing technical stakeholders, and independently executing both deliverables. I balanced this effort alongside my primary workload, which included scaling our Figma design system and leading design for core platform capabilities like BTC.

impact

the backstory

problem

Teams operated in parallel without a shared visual blueprint, which made aligning on the platform's overarching vision difficult. At the same time, overly technical architectural terminology made it hard for non-technical stakeholders to parse critical details, further compounding communication gaps.

As a result, onboarding new team members to the platform's high-level strategy and technical structure remained inconsistent across the organization.

process
Phase 1 — Discovery & Alignment
Facilitated working sessions with the Front-End Architect and Engineering Manager to extract core architectural principles in layman's terms.

I pulled together work from across the design team such as screens, concepts, and decisions that lived in separate files and separate heads, and assembled them into a single continuous picture.

For the first time, you could see everything in one place ... and a lot of the inconsistencies.

I gathered work designers were working on in one place to view

Rather than working from assumptions, I facilitated working sessions with both the Front-end Architect and an Engineering Manager (at that time the Product Design team was under Architecture and did not have a Head of Product Design) to extract and understand the most basic platform-level information that no one could answer:

  • What is the overall vision of the platform?
  • What are we actually building in layman's terms?
  • How do capabilities relate to one another?
  • How does this all connect?
  • What's the platform supposed to experience like on day one versus six months in?

To bridge the technical gap, I focused on absorbing complex technical details, asking key clarifying questions, and mapping them into accessible documentation for new team members and designers.

Phase 2 — Conceptual Analogy
Re-framed the platform as a house under construction (foundation, framing, electrical, plumbing) to clarify team dependencies and build sequence.

The first thing I created based on my findings was an analogy framework presentation.

I reframed the entire platform as a house being built from the ground up as it's one of the easiest things to picture. Everyone knows what a house is, everyone understands that you can't put the roof on before the walls.

By mapping platform capabilities to house architecture (foundation, framing, utilities) and team roles to specialized trades, abstract concepts became tangible. This framework clearly defined system dependencies, team ownership, and execution sequencing.

Once adopted, the framework enabled teams to dive deeper into platform discussions and served as a foundational reference for cross-functional onboarding.

It established a shared, accessible baseline that allowed non-technical stakeholders to engage effortlessly. For myself, it helped me answer where my projects like BTC fit in and its relationship to other services.
